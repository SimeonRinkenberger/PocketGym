What PocketGym stores

  • Your email address and password. Handled by Supabase Auth; the password is stored only as a salted hash and is never visible to us.
  • If you continue with Google or Apple, PocketGym receives the account identifier, name and email address the provider shares, together with tokens needed to authenticate the connection. Your provider password never reaches PocketGym. Apple lets you hide your real email address behind a private relay. For Apple sign-in, PocketGym also keeps a refresh token in server-only storage so it can revoke your Apple authorization when you delete your account.
  • The videos you save — the link, the creator name, the caption or description that was public on the post, and the workout extracted from it.
  • What you log — the sets, reps and weights you record in Workout Mode, plus any notes and planned days you add.
  • Your preferences and permission choices, including the date and version of your AI processing choice and optional reminder settings.
  • Your goal and body weight, if you give them — the goal you set (for example, get stronger or lose fat, and where you train) and, only if you enter it, your body weight with your last 52 weigh-ins. Clearing the body weight field removes all of it.
  • A personal save key so the iPhone share extension or an iOS Shortcut can add videos for you. You can replace it any time from Settings.
  • Your conversations with Pumpy, the coach, so you can pick a chat up where you left it, and the changes you confirmed from them. They are private to your account.
  • If you subscribe to PocketGym Plus, an identifier for you at your payment provider and the state of your subscription — the plan, whether it is running, and the date it renews or ends. Never your card. See Payments below.

What PocketGym does not do

  • No ads or advertising trackers, and no third-party web analytics scripts. PocketGym does keep the operational and subscription measurements described below.
  • Your library, logs and plan are private to your account and are not sold. Relevant details are processed by the service providers described below when you use AI features.
  • The PocketGym app does not collect your location. The ChatGPT plugin receives a rough location from ChatGPT with each request and does not read or keep it (see PocketGym in ChatGPT). Workout logs are fitness information, and anything you type into notes or Pumpy may include health or other sensitive information. Optional Apple Health and Apple Watch heart-rate access are described below.

Health information

What we treat as health information:

  • your body weight and your last 52 weigh-ins;
  • a weight-loss goal and its target;
  • anything you type about pain, injury, a medical condition, medication or pregnancy, in notes or to Pumpy;
  • the 1–10 effort rating on a finished workout.

Your workout log is fitness information, and we protect it the same way.

Safety markers. If a Pumpy message suggests a medical emergency or disordered eating, mentions medication or supplements, or someone under 18 asks to lose weight, Pumpy replies with a fixed safety message. We mark the message so the rule holds for the rest of the conversation.

Your explicit consent. We process health information only with your explicit consent. The AI permission PocketGym shows before your first AI request (in the app, or in the share sheet) asks for it in plain words: what you send can include health details you choose to share, such as injuries, pain, body weight and goals, and allowing AI processing means you consent to PocketGym and the AI providers it names processing them to coach you. We record the date and the version of the wording you agreed to, and when the wording changes we ask again. Body weight is optional: the field says it is saved to your account to tailor your coaching, and that you can clear it any time.

To withdraw consent or remove health information:

  • Settings › Data & privacy › AI processing › Turn off AI processing. Nothing more is sent to AI. Requests already sent and data already saved stay.
  • Delete single items. Clearing body weight removes all your weigh-ins. You can also delete a chat, a session or a saved workout.
  • Delete account removes everything.

Never for advertising. There are no ads and no trackers, and we don't sell health information or use it for marketing.

Apple Health and Apple Watch stay on your devices. On iPhone, PocketGym only writes finished workouts to Apple Health and never reads it. The Watch reads heart rate and active energy during a workout to show them on your watch and phone. Nothing from Apple Health, and no heart-rate value, goes to our servers or to AI.

Our server refuses requests to any other AI host. Reading a video on your iPhone uses Apple's models on the iPhone itself and sends nothing to these providers. See Reading on your iPhone.

PocketGym asks for permission before using AI features. You can decline and keep logging workouts and planning manually. In Settings, you can review or turn off AI processing. Turning it off stops future AI requests after the choice is saved; it does not recall requests already sent or delete saved workouts and conversations.

Pumpy on your iPhone. On an iPhone that can read videos itself, Add this video's tips to my plan (PocketGym Plus) is worked out on the iPhone. The iPhone reads the card and your next 7 planned days from your account. PocketGym's code ranks the days. Apple's on-device model then picks a day, picks which of the card's exercises to add, and writes one or two sentences. Only those choices, that sentence and the iOS version go to our server. The server builds the change from your saved card and keeps it in your Pumpy chats like any other. Nothing from this goes to OpenAI, Google or TypeSafe AI, and it does not need the AI permission. Nothing changes until you confirm.

Apple's Private Cloud Compute. If your plan is too long for the on-device model, the iPhone can send the same request to Apple's Private Cloud Compute instead: the card's exercises and a summary of your next 7 planned days. PocketGym asks you once before the first request, and you can turn it off in Settings › Data & privacy. Apple runs the request on its own servers and says it does not store it or make it available to anyone, including Apple; PocketGym's server receives only the choices and sentence described above. Apple sets a daily limit, which PocketGym shows wherever it is used. If Apple cannot be reached, the iPhone tries again on the device with a shorter plan. Apple's terms for Apple Intelligence apply.

To match exercises, sort shared links, rank searches and keep advice safe, PocketGym also sends TypeSafe AI's Jev model the text those decisions need: exercise names, post captions and descriptions, what you type into a search, and your Pumpy messages, which Jev screens for safety (a message can include health details you chose to type). Jev answers fixed questions about that text (for example, which exercise a name means, or whether a message needs the safety reply); it does not write replies and never receives images, audio, video or Apple Health data. PocketGym reaches Jev through Cloudflare Workers AI, which lists zero data retention for this model; PocketGym would reach Jev directly from TypeSafe AI only under a zero-data-retention agreement. TypeSafe AI states that it does not train on customer data (TypeSafe's terms). PocketGym keeps Jev's answers with a keyed fingerprint of the input to check its accuracy, never the text itself. Jev is used only after you allow the AI permission that names it.

When a person reads your data. No one reads your chats, except a Pumpy answer you report: we keep a copy of up to 32,000 characters so it can be reviewed and fixed. Safety screening only chooses whether Pumpy answers normally or with a fixed safety message; it makes no decision about you beyond that reply.

Other services involved

Every service provider named on this page receives only what it needs to do its job for PocketGym, and is bound by its terms with PocketGym to use that data only to provide its service and to protect it at least as well as this policy describes. None of them may sell it or use it for advertising. Strava, which you connect yourself, and the video platforms whose players you choose to play handle what you send them under their own policies.

Data is stored with Supabase in the United States. The ChatGPT plugin's front door and card pages run on Cloudflare. Sign-in codes and account emails are sent through Resend, which receives your email address and the message. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts and Fontshare.

Video playback happens in an embedded player from Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. Those players are the platforms' own and follow the platforms' privacy policies.

When you ask how an exercise is done, PocketGym shows a short demonstration clip. These come from a short list of fitness creators' public YouTube channels, collected in advance and stored by exercise, so most of the time nothing is looked up at all — the answer is already in PocketGym's own list, and more than one creator may be offered for the same movement. For a movement that list does not cover, the server falls back to searching YouTube, preferring those same channels. Either way only the name of the exercise is sent — never your email, your account or anything else about you — and any search result is cached and shared by everyone, so the same movement is looked up once for all of PocketGym rather than once per person. The thumbnail is loaded from Google's image host, and the clip, if you tap it, plays in a privacy-enhanced (no-cookie) YouTube player that is only added to the page once you tap and is removed again when you close the sheet. The link beside it opens YouTube's own search results.

Payments

In the native Android and iPhone apps, purchases are handled by Google Play or Apple. RevenueCat validates purchases and synchronizes subscription access with your PocketGym account. RevenueCat receives your PocketGym account ID, purchase history, product and transaction identifiers, subscription status, and technical information needed to process purchases. It does not receive your workout library or coaching conversations. Payment-card details stay with the store. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy.

Store subscriptions renew until cancelled in Google Play or Apple subscription settings. Deleting your PocketGym account or uninstalling the app does not cancel store subscriptions. Cancel in the store first if you want billing to stop. Store providers may retain transaction records required for tax, accounting, fraud prevention, and legal obligations.

PocketGym no longer sells subscriptions on the web. Web purchases through Stripe ended on 18 September 2026, and no web subscription bills anyone. PocketGym does not store your payment-card details. If your account has a record from Stripe's time, PocketGym keeps on it only the customer and subscription identifiers Stripe gave you, which plan it was and its status, the date the period ends, and the date a payment last failed, if one did; PocketGym's log of the notifications Stripe sent holds each one's identifier, type and when it was handled, and no account or payment details. Deleting your account removes the record from PocketGym and asks Stripe to delete the customer there. Stripe keeps its own record of payments under its own privacy policy, for as long as tax and accounting law says it must.

Connections

PocketGym can connect to Strava, if you choose to, so that a workout you have just finished can be sent there as an activity. You connect it yourself in Settings → Connections, and nothing happens until you do.

The connection is one-way. PocketGym writes to Strava and never reads from it: it does not see your activities, your friends, your routes, your segments or your profile, and no Strava data is ever given to any of the AI services above. What gets sent, and only when you tap Send to Strava on a session, is the workout's name, when you started it and how long it took, one line per exercise with the sets you logged, your total volume, any personal bests from that session, and a link back to PocketGym.

When you connect, Strava gives PocketGym's server two access keys. They are held on a row attached to your account on the server only — the app on your phone never receives them and cannot read them — together with your Strava athlete number and the identifier of each activity PocketGym created, so the same session is never posted twice. Disconnecting deletes both keys and tells Strava to forget PocketGym, and so does deleting your account. You can also revoke PocketGym yourself at any time from your Strava settings.

Apple Health, Apple Watch, widgets, Spotlight and reminders

Apple Health. If you turn on Save my workouts (Settings › Connections), PocketGym saves each workout you finish to the Health app on your iPhone: when it started and ended, any pauses, the kind of workout, the effort you rated and, when no Apple Watch recorded it and you have entered your body weight, an estimate of active energy. If you edit or remove that workout in PocketGym, PocketGym updates or removes the copy it saved. On iPhone, PocketGym only writes to Health; it never reads your Health data. Nothing from Health is sent to PocketGym's server or AI providers, used for advertising or marketing, or sold. Workouts already saved to Health stay there if you delete your PocketGym account; you can delete them in the Health app. You can turn saving off in PocketGym and manage Health permissions in your device's settings.

Apple Watch. The Apple Watch companion exchanges the current workout and workout controls with your paired iPhone. With Save my workouts on and Health access granted on the Watch, the Watch records the workout: it reads your heart rate and active energy while you train, saves the workout and your effort rating to Apple Health when you finish, and shares live heart rate with your iPhone so it can appear on your Lock Screen, during the workout and in its summary. Heart-rate values stay on your devices: PocketGym does not send them to its server or AI providers.

Effort. The 1–10 effort rating you give a finished workout is also saved with that workout in your PocketGym account, like the rest of your training log.

Widgets and Live Activities use a local shared container to display workout information, including on your Lock Screen or Dynamic Island when enabled. Anyone who can view those surfaces may see that information. While a workout runs, PocketGym keeps a record of your workout taps (and, with an Apple Watch, your heart rate) in files on your iPhone so these controls keep working when the app is closed. Those files are excluded from iCloud and computer backups; files older than a week are deleted the next time you start a workout, and all of them are removed if you delete the app. You control widgets, Live Activities and notification previews in your device settings.

Spotlight, Siri and Shortcuts. On iPhone, PocketGym keeps the names of your saved workouts, each with its category and number of exercises, in a file in the app and in the iPhone's own search index, so Spotlight, Siri and the Shortcuts app can find and open them. PocketGym does not send this list anywhere. While a workout is open, PocketGym tells the iPhone which one, so Siri can act on it if you ask. To remove the list, turn off Settings › Data & privacy › Show workouts in Spotlight. It is also removed when you sign out or delete your account.

If you enable reminders, PocketGym stores a push subscription or Apple push token, its association with your account, your timezone and reminder preferences, and delivery state. Apple's Push Notification service delivers iPhone notifications. You can turn reminders off in PocketGym Settings or disable notifications in your device settings. PocketGym does not use reminder tokens for advertising.

Reading on your iPhone

On an iPhone that can use Apple Intelligence, with Apple Intelligence turned on and iOS 27 or later, PocketGym can read a workout video on the iPhone itself. The video, its audio and its frames stay on your phone. Apple's speech recognition listens to the audio on the iPhone, Apple's Vision framework reads the text shown on screen, and Apple's on-device language model writes the workout card. When there are few words to go on, the model also looks at a few still frames. PocketGym's own code then checks every exercise and number against what the video says or shows and the post's caption, and leaves out what it cannot find. None of this uses OpenAI, Google or TypeSafe AI, and it does not need the AI permission described in AI and Pumpy.

This covers a video you pick from Photos or Files in PocketGym, a video file you share to PocketGym, a video you give PocketGym in Siri or Shortcuts, and a TikTok or Instagram link you add in PocketGym or share to it from another app. For a link, PocketGym's server first checks the shared cache below. If the cache has no card, the server fetches the post's public caption, creator name and cover image, as it does for any saved link. If the post has a video, your iPhone then downloads it from TikTok or Instagram, reads it and deletes it. A post with no video is read from its caption, on the iPhone. Every YouTube link, and any other web link, is read on our server as described in AI and Pumpy, which needs the AI permission.

It also covers one to ten pictures, such as a screenshot or a photo of a whiteboard or a printed program, that you pick in PocketGym or share to it. Apple's Vision framework reads the words in them and Apple's on-device model writes the card, on the iPhone. The pictures stay on your phone.

Apart from the link for the cache check and the video download above, what leaves your iPhone is the finished workout card: its title, category, exercises, sets, reps, times, weights, rest, short coaching cues, the moments in the video they come from, and a label saying it was read on an iPhone and on which iOS version. It is saved to your account like any other card, with the link, creator, caption and cover image when it came from a link. The exact words of speech or on-screen text behind each exercise are not sent. Cards read on your iPhone are never added to the shared cache.

The card can be wrong, so check the exercises and numbers before you train. Numbers the video did not state are left empty. If you tap Read it on our server instead, the video is read as described in AI and Pumpy, which needs the AI permission. To have new videos read on our server instead, turn off Settings › Data & privacy › Read videos on this iPhone. That choice is kept on that iPhone only.

Files and video reading

This section covers videos read on our server. A video read on your iPhone is covered in Reading on your iPhone.

Uploads enter private temporary storage. PocketGym's server sends supported audio or video to Google Gemini for transcription or workout extraction. PocketGym attempts to delete its temporary file and Google's uploaded file after processing, including failed attempts. A cleanup sweep targets files left in PocketGym storage after two hours; outages can delay cleanup. Extracted text and workout details remain on your saved card.

Pictures read on our server (on an iPhone that does not read them itself, or with Read videos on this iPhone turned off) are made smaller and stripped of location and camera details on your phone, and again on our server, before OpenAI reads the workout in them. PocketGym deletes them after the read, including failed attempts, and the same two-hour cleanup sweep targets any left behind. Pictures are never added to the shared cache.

For public links with little written workout information, PocketGym may also read available audio or video, automatically or when you request it. Source material fetched from a public post may contribute to the shared cache below. Your private uploads and supplied captions or HTML are excluded from new shared cache entries.

Deleting a temporary uploaded file does not erase a provider's separate processing or abuse monitoring records. Provider handling depends on the account and service configuration; see OpenAI's data controls and Google's API terms. In particular, Google's paid and unpaid services have different data-use terms.

Shared extraction cache

When a video is read for the first time, the resulting workout is cached under the video's public ID so the next person who saves the same video gets it instantly and no AI call is needed. Cards read on an iPhone are never added to it. That cache holds only information taken from the public post — never anything about who saved it. It is not linked to any account, so deleting an account does not remove it. An entry not updated for 18 months is deleted; a creator can ask us to remove one sooner (see the terms). The ChatGPT plugin keeps its own cards, apart from this cache. See PocketGym in ChatGPT.

PocketGym in ChatGPT

PocketGym is also a plugin inside ChatGPT. This section covers the plugin only. The rest of this page covers the PocketGym app and website. Using the plugin needs no PocketGym account, and it is not linked to one. ChatGPT is run by OpenAI, and OpenAI's own policies cover what ChatGPT keeps.

What the plugin receives

  • Links you share. A link to a TikTok, an Instagram Reel, a YouTube video or a web page.
  • Workout text you share. A caption you paste, text ChatGPT reads from a screenshot you share, the exercises ChatGPT writes down from a video file you attach to your message or from a video's frames (below), or your own description of a workout, up to 4,000 characters at a time. ChatGPT watches a video file you attach; PocketGym receives only the text ChatGPT writes, never the file. Please share only the workout.
  • Your words for an adjustment, such as "dumbbells only, 25 minutes" (up to 500 characters). We use them to check the adjusted card and do not keep them.
  • The card ChatGPT drafts. The exercises, sets, reps, rest and weights it sends to PocketGym to be checked, with a short quote from the source text for each exercise.
  • Anonymous IDs. ChatGPT sends an anonymized user ID and an anonymized conversation ID with each request. They are not your name, email or ChatGPT account. We use them to count and limit requests and to keep a private card with the person who made it. We never store them as received. We store only a one-way hash made with a secret value that only PocketGym's server holds. To stop one source flooding the plugin, we also keep a salted hash of the network address of the server that called us, which is usually OpenAI's server and not your device. If ChatGPT sends neither ID, that hash stands in for them in the counts, and no card is tied to it. The address itself is not stored.
  • Things ChatGPT sends that we do not use. Your language, a rough location (city, region, country and time zone) and a description of your browser or app arrive with each request. PocketGym does not read or keep them.

The plugin does not ask for, and we do not collect, your name, email, phone number, password, payment details, ID numbers, precise location, body weight, health records or your ChatGPT conversation.

Videos read from their frames

When the written text of a TikTok video or an Instagram reel holds no workout, PocketGym may read the video from its frames when you ask about it. Our server fetches the post's public video, the way a browser would, and Cloudflare, which runs that server, cuts 6 to 16 still frames from it. The frames go to ChatGPT in your conversation, where ChatGPT reads them, and what ChatGPT writes down from them is handled like text you share (above). We keep neither the video nor the frames: they exist only in memory while your request is answered, and they are never stored, cached or shown on a card page. Only the post's public link is fetched; nothing about you is sent to the platform. This can be switched off at any time and has daily limits.

Why we use it

  • To turn a link into a workout card, check it, show it, let you adjust it and open it as a web page.
  • To show a card built only from public post text to the next person who shares the same link, so the post is not read again.
  • To keep within each platform's limits and stop one person using up the day.
  • To count how many people use the plugin and whether they come back (see Counts below).

We do not use plugin data for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not use it to train AI models. PocketGym's server makes no AI calls for the plugin. ChatGPT, which is OpenAI's AI, writes the card. The plugin does not use the AI providers listed under AI and Pumpy above.

Who receives it

  • OpenAI, which runs ChatGPT, receives what you type and share in ChatGPT, and what PocketGym sends back: the card text and, while a card is being drafted, the numbered lines of the post's text, and the still frames of a video read from its frames. OpenAI handles that under its own terms and privacy policy.
  • Supabase (United States) hosts the database and the function that runs the plugin.
  • Cloudflare delivers the plugin's front door and the card pages, passes requests on to Supabase and makes the address hash described above. When a video is read from its frames, our server on Cloudflare fetches the post's public video and Cloudflare's media service cuts the frames; neither is kept. Supabase and Cloudflare act on our behalf and keep their own request logs.
  • TikTok, Instagram, YouTube (Google) and the website you shared a link to. When we read a post, our server asks for the post's public page or public data. The platform sees a request from PocketGym's server for that link. We send nothing about you.

No one else receives it. See also Other services involved above.

Card pages

A card page exists only after you tap the button on a card. Anyone with the link can open it. The link is a random 10-character code and the page asks search engines not to list it, but treat it like any link you share. The page shows the card, credits the creator, links to the original post and links to PocketGym's page on the App Store. A card built from text you shared, or adjusted by ChatGPT, says so on the page. The page sets no cookies and loads nothing from other sites. We count page views and taps on its buttons by card, with nothing about the visitor. Cloudflare, which delivers the page, sees the visitor's IP address as any web host does.

Opening a card in the app

When PocketGym is installed, a card page's link opens the card in the app, where you can save it to Workouts. If that link is the first link a new install of the app opens, within a day of installing, and your PocketGym account is new (made within a day of that link), the account notes once that you came from ChatGPT, with the card's 10-character link code and the date. We use it only to count how many people start using PocketGym from ChatGPT. It sits in your account settings, is part of Export my data and is deleted with your account. Nothing about your ChatGPT conversation reaches the app, and no other app or service is told.

Counts

The plugin counts what happens so we can run it and see whether it works. A count is an event name (for example read, card shown, follow-along opened or finished, page viewed, App Store link tapped), the time, the card or post it relates to, the hashed ID when there is one, and the outcome (for example read, paused or limit reached). Counts hold no text from you. We use the hashed ID to tell how many different people use the plugin and how many come back a week later. We do not link counts to an account, build profiles from them or use them for advertising, and we cannot tell who a hashed ID belongs to.

  • Reads, counts and limit counters delete themselves on the schedule above. They are tied to a hashed ID that you cannot see, so we cannot pull out one person's records on request. The access, correction and deletion rights under Your rights apply to what we can find, which is a card or page you identify.
  • Email business@quarterdeckcollective.com and write "Privacy" in the subject line.

Taking your data with you

Settings → Data & privacy → Export my data writes everything you put into PocketGym — your workout cards, every session you have logged, your plan, your collections, your goals and their programs, the corrections you made to cards, your records and awards, your chats with Pumpy and any Pumpy answers you reported, your plan status (store and subscription records, without payment details), your reminder settings and your settings — into a single JSON file on your device. It is a plain, structured, machine-readable format, so you can read it yourself or move it somewhere else. Your personal save key, reminder tokens and addresses, and connection keys (Strava, Apple) are deliberately left out of the file, because each works as a key and files get forwarded.

Deleting your account

Removing a workout deletes it immediately. To delete your whole account, open PocketGym and go to Settings → Data & privacy → Delete account. You will be asked to type DELETE to confirm, and then the following are removed from the active app:

  • your workout cards, and any video files you uploaded;
  • every session you have logged, and your plan;
  • your collections and the corrections you made to cards;
  • your chats with Pumpy;
  • your profile, your settings and your personal save key;
  • your subscription record in PocketGym, and any customer record left at Stripe from the old web purchases (PocketGym asks Stripe to delete it). Google Play and Apple subscriptions must be cancelled in the store separately;
  • your Strava connection, if you made one — Strava is told to forget PocketGym and both access keys are deleted;
  • your customer record at RevenueCat — PocketGym asks RevenueCat to delete it, and keeps asking until RevenueCat confirms;
  • your Apple sign-in authorization, if connected — PocketGym asks Apple to revoke the stored grants and then deletes those tokens. You may need to sign in with Apple again first if the account was connected before token revocation was available;
  • the sign-in account itself.

Export your data first if you want to keep it. Account deletion cannot be undone in the app. Backup copies and provider security records may remain under their separate retention policies. PocketGym keeps its own record of what the AI cost to run for 13 months, and the deletion strips your account id out of those rows rather than keeping them attached to you. New AI reservation records are retained for up to 90 days; request admission records for up to 40 days, with scheduled cleanup.

If you cannot sign in, email business@quarterdeckcollective.com from your account's address and it will be erased for you.
