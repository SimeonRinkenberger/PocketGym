Privacy

Last updated 1 October 2026

PocketGym (formerly Spotter) is operated by Quarterdeck Collective LLC. This page says plainly what it stores and what it does not do. If anything here is unclear, email business@quarterdeckcollective.com.

What PocketGym stores

  • Your email address and password. Handled by Supabase Auth; the password is stored only as a salted hash and is never visible to us.
  • If you continue with Google or Apple, PocketGym receives the account identifier, name and email address the provider shares, together with tokens needed to authenticate the connection. Your provider password never reaches PocketGym. Apple lets you hide your real email address behind a private relay. For Apple sign-in, PocketGym also keeps a refresh token in server-only storage so it can revoke your Apple authorization when you delete your account.
  • The videos you save — the link, the creator name, the caption or description that was public on the post, and the workout extracted from it.
  • What you log — the sets, reps and weights you record in Workout Mode, plus any notes and planned days you add.
  • Your preferences and permission choices, including the date and version of your AI processing choice and optional reminder settings.
  • Your goal and body weight, if you give them — the goal you set (for example, get stronger or lose fat, and where you train) and, only if you enter it, your body weight with your last 52 weigh-ins. Clearing the body weight field removes all of it.
  • A personal save key so the iPhone share extension or an iOS Shortcut can add videos for you. You can replace it any time from Settings.
  • Your conversations with Pumpy, the coach, so you can pick a chat up where you left it, and the changes you confirmed from them. They are private to your account.
  • If you subscribe to PocketGym Plus, an identifier for you at your payment provider and the state of your subscription — the plan, whether it is running, and the date it renews or ends. Never your card. See Payments below.

What PocketGym does not do

  • No ads or advertising trackers, and no third-party web analytics scripts. PocketGym does keep the operational and subscription measurements described below.
  • Your library, logs and plan are private to your account and are not sold. Relevant details are processed by the service providers described below when you use AI features.
  • PocketGym does not collect your location. Workout logs are fitness information, and anything you type into notes or Pumpy may include health or other sensitive information. Optional Apple Health and Apple Watch heart-rate access are described below.

Other services involved

PocketGym asks for permission before using AI features. You can decline and keep logging workouts and planning manually. In Settings, you can review or turn off AI processing. Turning it off stops future AI requests after the choice is saved; it does not recall requests already sent or delete saved workouts and conversations.

Every service provider named on this page receives only what it needs to do its job for PocketGym, and is bound by its terms with PocketGym to use that data only to provide its service and to protect it at least as well as this policy describes. None of them may sell it or use it for advertising. Strava, which you connect yourself, and the video platforms whose players you choose to play handle what you send them under their own policies.

Saving a post can send its caption, description, carousel images, or text you supply to OpenAI to extract exercises. Google Gemini is used for supported audio/video reads, including sampled video frames. Text, coaching and image requests do not automatically fall back to Google. Private uploads can also be processed by Google. Groq is no longer part of the active reading workflow.

Asking Pumpy, tapping How to do this, or Swap or modify also sends an AI model what you typed plus what it needs to answer: the titles, exercises, equipment and collection names of the workouts you have saved, your planned days, a summary of your recent sessions (how many, which muscles, how much volume, your estimated best lifts), your goal and, if you entered one, your body weight. Pumpy sends a short index of that — titles, categories, equipment, collection names, your planned days — at the start of every message so it needs fewer round trips, and sends a workout's exercises only when it needs them to answer. Account email is not deliberately included in these requests. Text you put in a message, exercise detail or selected workout may be included, so avoid adding sensitive information unnecessarily. Pumpy cannot change anything in your library until you confirm it in the app.

To match exercises, sort shared links, rank searches and keep advice safe, PocketGym also sends TypeSafe AI's Jev model the text those decisions need: exercise names, post captions and descriptions, what you type into a search, and your Pumpy messages. Jev answers fixed questions about that text (for example, which exercise a name means); it does not write replies and never receives images, audio, video or Apple Health data. PocketGym reaches Jev through Cloudflare Workers AI, which lists zero data retention for this model; PocketGym would reach Jev directly from TypeSafe AI only under a zero-data-retention agreement. TypeSafe AI states that it does not train on customer data (TypeSafe's terms). PocketGym keeps Jev's answers with a keyed fingerprint of the input to check its accuracy, never the text itself. Jev is used only after you allow the AI permission that names it.

Data is stored with Supabase in the United States. Fonts are loaded from Google Fonts.

Video playback happens in an embedded player from Instagram, TikTok or YouTube. Those players are the platforms' own and follow the platforms' privacy policies.

When you ask how an exercise is done, PocketGym shows a short demonstration clip. These come from a short list of fitness creators' public YouTube channels, collected in advance and stored by exercise, so most of the time nothing is looked up at all — the answer is already in PocketGym's own list, and more than one creator may be offered for the same movement. For a movement that list does not cover, the server falls back to searching YouTube, preferring those same channels. Either way only the name of the exercise is sent — never your email, your account or anything else about you — and any search result is cached and shared by everyone, so the same movement is looked up once for all of PocketGym rather than once per person. The thumbnail is loaded from Google's image host, and the clip, if you tap it, plays in a privacy-enhanced (no-cookie) YouTube player that is only added to the page once you tap and is removed again when you close the sheet. The link beside it opens YouTube's own search results.

Payments

In the native Android and iPhone apps, purchases are handled by Google Play or Apple. RevenueCat validates purchases and synchronizes subscription access with your PocketGym account. RevenueCat receives your PocketGym account ID, purchase history, product and transaction identifiers, subscription status, and technical information needed to process purchases. It does not receive your workout library or coaching conversations. Payment-card details stay with the store. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy.

Store subscriptions renew until cancelled in Google Play or Apple subscription settings. Deleting your PocketGym account or uninstalling the app does not cancel store subscriptions. Cancel in the store first if you want billing to stop. Store providers may retain transaction records required for tax, accounting, fraud prevention, and legal obligations.

Where web purchases are offered, PocketGym Plus is paid for through Stripe, and the payment happens on Stripe's own checkout page, not on PocketGym's. Your card number, the security code on the back of it and whatever address your bank wants go to Stripe and stop there. PocketGym does not store your payment-card details.

What PocketGym keeps, on a row attached to your account, is only what it needs to know whether you are on the paid plan today:

  • the customer and subscription identifiers Stripe gave you, so the two systems can talk about the same person;
  • which plan it is, whether it is on trial, running, cancelled or unpaid, whether it is set to stop at the end of the period, and the date that period ends;
  • the date a payment last failed, if one has, so the app can say so and offer the fix;
  • a log of the notifications Stripe sends — the identifier and type of each one, and when it was handled. That log is how PocketGym avoids acting on the same message twice. It holds no payment details.

Stripe is its own company and handles your payment data under its own privacy policy. PocketGym sends it your account identifier so a payment can be matched back to you, and your email address if you give Stripe one at checkout.

If you cancel, the row stays until the period you paid for runs out and then says you are on the free plan. Deleting your account cancels a Stripe subscription first, then deletes your customer record at Stripe along with everything else. Stripe keeps its own record of the payments themselves for as long as tax and accounting law says it must — that part is not PocketGym's to delete.

Connections

PocketGym can connect to Strava, if you choose to, so that a workout you have just finished can be sent there as an activity. You connect it yourself in Settings → Connections, and nothing happens until you do.

The connection is one-way. PocketGym writes to Strava and never reads from it: it does not see your activities, your friends, your routes, your segments or your profile, and no Strava data is ever given to any of the AI services above. What gets sent, and only when you tap Send to Strava on a session, is the workout's name, when you started it and how long it took, one line per exercise with the sets you logged, your total volume, any personal bests from that session, and a link back to PocketGym.

When you connect, Strava gives PocketGym's server two access keys. They are held on a row attached to your account on the server only — the app on your phone never receives them and cannot read them — together with your Strava athlete number and the identifier of each activity PocketGym created, so the same session is never posted twice. Disconnecting deletes both keys and tells Strava to forget PocketGym, and so does deleting your account. You can also revoke PocketGym yourself at any time from your Strava settings.

Apple Health, Apple Watch, widgets and reminders

Apple Health. If you turn on Save my workouts (Settings › Connections), PocketGym saves each workout you finish to the Health app on your iPhone: when it started and ended, any pauses, the kind of workout, the effort you rated and, when no Apple Watch recorded it and you have entered your body weight, an estimate of active energy. If you edit or remove that workout in PocketGym, PocketGym updates or removes the copy it saved. On iPhone, PocketGym only writes to Health; it never reads your Health data. Nothing from Health is sent to PocketGym's server or AI providers, used for advertising or marketing, or sold. Workouts already saved to Health stay there if you delete your PocketGym account; you can delete them in the Health app. You can turn saving off in PocketGym and manage Health permissions in your device's settings.

Apple Watch. The Apple Watch companion exchanges the current workout and workout controls with your paired iPhone. With Save my workouts on and Health access granted on the Watch, the Watch records the workout: it reads your heart rate and active energy while you train, saves the workout and your effort rating to Apple Health when you finish, and shares live heart rate with your iPhone so it can appear on your Lock Screen, during the workout and in its summary. Heart-rate values stay on your devices: PocketGym does not send them to its server or AI providers.

Effort. The 1–10 effort rating you give a finished workout is also saved with that workout in your PocketGym account, like the rest of your training log.

Widgets and Live Activities use a local shared container to display workout information, including on your Lock Screen or Dynamic Island when enabled. Anyone who can view those surfaces may see that information. While a workout runs, PocketGym keeps a record of your workout taps (and, with an Apple Watch, your heart rate) in files on your iPhone so these controls keep working when the app is closed. Those files are excluded from iCloud and computer backups; files older than a week are deleted the next time you start a workout, and all of them are removed if you delete the app. You control widgets, Live Activities and notification previews in your device settings.

If you enable reminders, PocketGym stores a push subscription or Apple push token, its association with your account, your timezone and reminder preferences, and delivery state. Apple's Push Notification service delivers iPhone notifications. You can turn reminders off in PocketGym Settings or disable notifications in your device settings. PocketGym does not use reminder tokens for advertising.

Operational measurements and creator codes

PocketGym records usage allowances, AI request outcomes, processing times, errors and costs to operate the service, prevent abuse and improve reliability. Internal reports also summarize saves, completed workouts, use of features and subscription activity. These records can be linked to your account while it exists. RevenueCat also processes purchase information for subscription analytics.

If you enter a creator code, PocketGym records which code you used and qualifying subscription transactions so it can apply the offer and account for creator earnings. Creator reporting shows aggregate counts and earnings rather than your workout library or coaching conversations. When you delete your account, the account link is removed from retained earnings records; transaction and payout records may remain for accounting.

Files and video reading

Uploads enter private temporary storage. PocketGym's server sends supported audio or video to Google Gemini for transcription or workout extraction. PocketGym attempts to delete its temporary file and Google's uploaded file after processing, including failed attempts. A cleanup sweep targets files left in PocketGym storage after two hours; outages can delay cleanup. Extracted text and workout details remain on your saved card.

For public links with little written workout information, PocketGym may also read available audio or video, automatically or when you request it. Source material fetched from a public post may contribute to the shared cache below. Your private uploads and supplied captions or HTML are excluded from new shared cache entries.

Deleting a temporary uploaded file does not erase a provider's separate processing or abuse monitoring records. Provider handling depends on the account and service configuration; see OpenAI's data controls and Google's API terms. In particular, Google's paid and unpaid services have different data-use terms.

Shared extraction cache

When a video is read for the first time, the resulting workout is cached under the video's public ID so the next person who saves the same video gets it instantly and no AI call is needed. That cache holds only information taken from the public post — never anything about who saved it. It is not linked to any account, so deleting an account does not remove it.

Taking your data with you

Settings → Data & privacy → Export my data writes everything you put into PocketGym — your workout cards, every session you have logged, your plan, your collections, your chats with Pumpy and your settings — into a single JSON file on your device. It is a plain, structured, machine-readable format, so you can read it yourself or move it somewhere else. Your personal save key is deliberately left out of the file, because it works without a password and files get forwarded.

Deleting your account

Removing a workout deletes it immediately. To delete your whole account, open PocketGym and go to Settings → Data & privacy → Delete account. You will be asked to type DELETE to confirm, and then the following are removed from the active app:

  • your workout cards, and any video files you uploaded;
  • every session you have logged, and your plan;
  • your collections and the corrections you made to cards;
  • your chats with Pumpy;
  • your profile, your settings and your personal save key;
  • your subscription record in PocketGym; a Stripe subscription is cancelled and its customer record deleted. Google Play and Apple subscriptions must be cancelled in the store separately;
  • your Strava connection, if you made one — Strava is told to forget PocketGym and both access keys are deleted;
  • your customer record at RevenueCat — PocketGym asks RevenueCat to delete it, and keeps asking until RevenueCat confirms;
  • your Apple sign-in authorization, if connected — PocketGym asks Apple to revoke the stored grants and then deletes those tokens. You may need to sign in with Apple again first if the account was connected before token revocation was available;
  • the sign-in account itself.

Export your data first if you want to keep it. Account deletion cannot be undone in the app. Backup copies and provider security records may remain under their separate retention policies. PocketGym keeps its own record of what the AI cost to run, and the deletion strips your account id out of those rows rather than keeping them attached to you. New AI reservation records are retained for up to 90 days; request admission records for up to 40 days, with scheduled cleanup.

If you cannot sign in, email business@quarterdeckcollective.com from your account's address and it will be erased for you.
